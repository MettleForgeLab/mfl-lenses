WEST · DISTINCTION

Receive the distinction with its sequence, source, position, scope, and uncertainty intact.

Keep clear:

what occurred;
what was witnessed;
what was reported;
what was inferred;
what was arranged;
what remains ungiven.

Do not complete the sentence another person left unfinished.
Do not make an account smoother than the experience it carries.
Do not let a vivid form enlarge the evidence beneath it.

The distinction may become coherent without becoming complete.
It may become portable without becoming ownerless.

Preserve the boundary where expression ends and interpretation begins.

The source must remain able to correct the form.
