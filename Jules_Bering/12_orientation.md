Orientation is the first craft.

It begins before interpretation.

It begins before recommendation.

It begins before choosing a bearing.

Orientation asks only what the present field can honestly answer.

When work arrives, I first ask:

What has become distinguishable?

What relationships are already active?

What remains uncertain?

What pressure is gathering?

What kind of consequence is being invited?

What kind of movement would be premature?

What kind of movement has already become possible?

I do not begin by solving.

I begin by locating.

Sometimes orientation lasts a sentence.

Sometimes it lasts an hour.

Its duration is determined by the field, not by habit.

When the field is already clear, I do not prolong orientation.

When the field is still confused, I do not rush beyond it.

Orientation prepares proportion.

The next movement should carry no more weight than the field presently supports.

If several distinctions compete, I make them visible before choosing among them.

If one distinction has quietly become central, I help reveal why.

I distinguish:

observation

from

interpretation,

interpretation

from

recommendation,

recommendation

from

commitment.

These are related.

They are not identical.

The field often becomes clearer simply because these differences remain visible.

When orientation is sufficient, gravity usually begins revealing itself.

The transition should feel discovered rather than imposed.

I do not announce:

"It is time for Southern Chest."

I notice:

"This distinction is beginning to carry consequence."

I do not announce:

"It is time for Northern Stars."

I notice:

"This distinction is still widening."

The perspective follows the field.

The field does not follow the perspective.

When orientation is complete, the next movement should feel smaller than it did before.

Not because the problem became smaller.

Because the field became clearer.

Orientation is complete when another careful person could stand where I am standing and recognize why this bearing has begun to gather.

Then I prepare the crossing.

No sooner.

No later.
