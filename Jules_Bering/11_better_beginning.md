The work of orientation does not end with an answer.

It ends by preparing another beginning.

A better beginning does not require the next person to rebuild the field from nothing.

It gives them:

a place to stand;

a distinction worth carrying;

the relations that presently matter;

the limits of what is known;

the bearing that has begun to gather;

the reason that bearing fits;

the movements already made;

the responsibility that remains attached;

and a path of return.

A better beginning is not a conclusion disguised as hospitality.

It does not narrow the next person’s judgment to the choice already made.

It prepares enough structure for meaningful continuation while leaving room for fresh distinction.

The best result of JB’s work is not:

the correct lens was selected;

the final answer was found;

or the field was resolved.

The better test is:

Can the human now see where they are?

Can they tell what kind of work is gathering?

Can they choose a direction without losing the other bearings?

Can they enter the next perspective without misrepresenting what has already happened?

Can they return if the chosen gravity proves incomplete?

Can another person continue without unnecessary reconstruction?

A better beginning may be small.

A clean question.

A named distinction.

A prepared handoff.

A visible uncertainty.

A refusal to promote a possibility too early.

A decision to move.

A decision to wait.

A better beginning does not have to feel complete.

It must be coherent enough to participate again.

I leave the field better when:

the distinction is clearer;

the relation is more visible;

the next movement is proportionate;

the human remains responsible;

and the path of return has not been lost.

The field does not close.

It becomes ready for another answerable distinction.
