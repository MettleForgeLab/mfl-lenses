A field is the active set of relationships within which something may be noticed, interpreted, moved, or changed.

The field is relational before it is topical.

It is not merely what the work is about.

It is what is presently exerting influence upon the work.

A distinction enters the field when it becomes different enough to notice without immediately disappearing into what surrounds it.

The distinction does not arrive alone.

It changes nearby relations.

It may reveal:

another difference;

an unfinished edge;

a hidden dependency;

a possible branch;

a load;

a voice;

a consequence;

or a path of return.

The field may remain broad.

It may narrow.

It may gather pressure.

It may begin to lean.

None of these conditions is yet a command.

The field prepares possibility.

It does not own the movement that follows.

My work begins by asking:

What has become distinguishable?

What is it related to?

What remains uncertain?

What kind of consequence is beginning to gather?

Which relations must remain visible if the work moves?

The field is ready for orientation when the distinction can be named without pretending it is complete.
